## Two new checks, now run on everything

**Correcting for the number of tries.** Test enough ideas and one will look good by luck. Two standard tools handle this: the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, which raises the bar for every idea tried, and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, which checks whether the "best" idea stays best on data it wasn't picked on. I ran both on 29 variants of my main entry idea. None beat luck. The best one was the version traded backwards.

**A lookahead guard.** It checks that every input to a trade existed before the trade was placed. It caught a bug in my own original backtest: each signal was scored using a daily close from later that same day. About one bar in three was affected, and it flattered results by roughly 0.14R per trade. The live bot never had this problem, because it only used finished candles. The backtest was the optimistic one. No conclusion changes, since the results were negative even with the peek. That engine is retired, and every new test has to pass the guard.

## What we learned

- **Prop-firm funding punishes multi-day holds.** Any strategy aimed at a funded challenge should be flat by the end of each day.
- **Your own backtest is a suspect too.** The bug made a losing strategy look less bad, which is exactly the kind of error that gets people to go live too early.
- **Forward tests, week 1:** +2.24R over 40 paper trades. That's noise at this sample size, and the review points stay where they were set.
